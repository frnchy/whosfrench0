# Research: "What if everyone turned 6 for 5 seconds?"

Location: **a Times Square intersection, New York City**, at rush hour.
Slow motion: the 5 real seconds run as about 40 s of screen time (8× slow). The HUD shows a real-time clock (T+0.00 s → T+5.00 s) ticking slowly, plus "SECOND n / 5".

Framing rule: every effect comes from **body size**, which is measurable. Nothing claims how a 6-year-old *thinks* or *behaves* (that can't be sourced inside 5 seconds).
Labels: **[S]** sourced, **[D]** derived by arithmetic from sourced numbers, **[E]** estimated (it must be labelled "estimated" on screen, or cut).

## The core numbers (HUD)

| | Before (adults 20+) | After (age 6) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Height | men 68.9 in, women 63.5 in → **avg 5′6″** (66.2 in) [S1] | boys 116.0 cm, girls 115.1 cm → **avg 3′10″** (45.5 in) [S2] | **−31 %** [D] |
| Weight | men 199.0 lb, women 171.8 lb → **avg 185 lb** [S1] | boys 20.5 kg, girls 20.2 kg → **avg 45 lb** [S3] | **−76 %** [D] |
| Grip strength | peak median: men 51 kg, women 31 kg [S4] | about 7–8 kg at age 6 (girls 67 N, boys 82 N) [S5] | about **−80 %** [D] |

**HUD metric: AVERAGE HEIGHT, 5′6″ → 3′10″.** It snaps at T+0.00 and snaps back at T+5.00.

## Second by second

| Real time | Effect | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| T+0.00 | Every person on the street drops about 20 inches (5′6″ → 3′10″) and loses three-quarters of their weight | S/D | S1, S2, S3 |
| T+0.0–1.0 | Clothes and shoes are now adult-sized on child bodies: pants slide, shoes slip off mid-stride | D | follows from S1–S3 |
| Second 1 | Grip drops about 80 %: shopping bags, coffees and briefcases fall | D | S4, S5 |
| Second 1 | Phones held up to faces don't unlock (Face ID was trained on an adult face) | **E** | Apple says Face ID adapts to gradual changes like makeup or facial hair, and that it's less reliable "among children under the age of 13, because their distinct facial features might not have fully developed" [S6]. A whole new face is our inference, so **estimated**. |
| Second 2 | Drivers: seat belts no longer fit. Adult belts fit "typically when children reach about 4 feet 9 inches", and 3′10″ is 11 inches short, so the shoulder belt crosses the neck and the lap belt crosses the stomach | S/D | S7 |
| Second 2 | Feet no longer reach the pedals, so nobody is on the brake or the gas | **E** | estimated from height (no sourced pedal-reach figure) |
| Second 3 | Traffic keeps rolling: at NYC's 25 mph default, a car covers **36.7 ft every second** and **183 ft in the 5 seconds** | S/D | S8; 25 mph × 5 s |
| Second 3–4 | The twist: most new cars brake **themselves**. 20 automakers fitted automatic emergency braking to at least 95 % of their light vehicles (Sept 2022–Aug 2023), "typically with pedestrian detection" | S | S9 |
| Second 4 (hero shot) | A crosswalk full of 3-foot people in giant clothes. The new SUV autobrakes, but the city bus beside it (heavier than the 8,500 lb pledge covered) keeps rolling | S/D | S9 (pledge covers vehicles "up to 8,500 pounds") |
| Second 4 | A fender-bender sets off a front air bag in front of a 6-year-old driver. That's why "all children younger than 13 years should ride in the back seat" | S | S7 |
| Second 5 | Peak chaos, then everyone snaps back to adult size, barefoot, holding up their pants, with the bus 183 ft further down the road | D | S8 maths |
| After | The surprising last fact: their **heads barely shrank**. "By age 5 years brain size is approximately 90% of adult size." The low-poly kids will have big heads, which is accurate. | S | S10 |

**Cut (no source):** no "kids panic / cry / run" behaviour, and no injuries shown. Collisions are slow-speed bumps into props (a hot-dog cart, a planter, a lamp post), never into people.

**Closing question (two-sided):** *"Who's in more danger: the kids walking, or the kids driving? 👇"*
