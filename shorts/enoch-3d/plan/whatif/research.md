# Research — « Et si les 200 anges revenaient ce soir ? » (1 Hénoch 6–10)

A what-if: the modern setting, the views, apps, deliveries and the Uber joke are fiction and presented as such
(« Heureusement, ce n'est jamais arrivé »). Every story beat it borrows comes from R. H. Charles, *The Book of Enoch* (1912/1917):

| Beat | Text (Charles) | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 200 angels descend on Mount Hermon | 6:6 "they were in all two hundred; who descended in the days of Jared on the summit of Mount Hermon" | textuel |
| Azazel teaches men to forge swords | 8:1 "Azazel taught men to make swords, and knives, and shields, and breastplates" | textuel |
| Kokabiel and astrology / the stars | 8:3 "Kôkabêl the constellations … Baraqîjâl taught astrology" | textuel (« version premium » : humour) |
| Giants of three thousand ells (≈ 1 350 m at ≈ 0.45 m/ell) | 7:2 "great giants, whose height was three thousand ells" | textuel (one week later : dramatisation) |
| They devour the harvests, then everything | 7:3–5 "consumed all the acquisitions of men … turned against them" | textuel |
| The earth cries out | 7:6; 8:4 "as men perished, they cried, and their cry went up to heaven" | textuel |
| Four archangels come down; Raphael binds Azazel | 9:1 (Michael, Uriel, Raphael, Gabriel); 10:4 "Bind Azazel hand and foot" | textuel |
